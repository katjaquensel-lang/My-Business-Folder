# Hook and Subject-Line Library

> Openers are most of the game. A great piece with a weak opener never gets read.
> These are fill-in PATTERNS, not finished lines. The skill adapts them to the member's voice and
> her one person, fills the brackets with the member's REAL detail, and never ships a bracketed line.
> No invented numbers or clients. If a pattern needs a number the member does not have, pick another.

## How to use this file
Generate several openers from different patterns, then score them against the Opener Test below and
keep the strongest. Never hand over the first one. Variety then selection beats one safe guess.

## The Opener Test (every hook and subject line must pass)
- [ ] Specific, not vague. Contains a concrete detail, number, or named moment, not "success" / "growth."
- [ ] Opens a real curiosity gap the piece actually pays off.
- [ ] Sounds like the member talking, not a brand announcing.
- [ ] Could not be the opener of a hundred other businesses. If it could, it is too generic.

---

## Subject-line patterns (25 to 55 characters, one job: get the open)

- The thought already in her head: "[her exact 11pm question]"
- The honest confession: "I [admission a brand normally hides]"
- The specific number that promises a story: "[odd specific number] [thing]"
- The pattern interrupt: "[a statement that contradicts what she expects]"
- The personal-message disguise (reads like a friend, not a broadcast): "quick thing about [topic]"
- The open loop: "the [thing] nobody tells you about [topic]"
- The named enemy (the outside cause, not her): "it was never [what she blames]"

Preview text rule: the preview EXTENDS the subject, it never repeats it. Subject opens the loop,
preview widens it, the email closes it.

## Hook patterns (first 1-3 seconds of a post, first line of a page)

- Her exact words: lead with the sentence she would say herself, verbatim.
- The contrarian truth: "[the thing everyone in the space believes] is wrong. here is what actually [result]."
  (Write it in the member's voice, NOT in the banned "it's not X, it's Y" shape.)
- The specific number: "[precise number] [surprising context]."
- The confession: "[the embarrassing or honest admission] that [led to the result]."
- The named moment: "[a scene with a time and place she has lived]."
- The stakes question: "[the question whose answer she needs]?"
- The reframe opener: name what she blames, then hint there is a hidden cause (no cliche construction).

## Why hooks FAIL (the skill checks its own hook against these)
- It describes instead of dropping her into a moment. ("Here are some tips for..." is a description.)
- It is about the member, not the reader. (Starts with "I" and stays there.)
- It is vague enough to belong to any business.
- It promises curiosity the piece never pays off.
- It front-loads context she has not earned yet. Cut the throat-clearing; start at the moment.

## Carousel-specific (slides shown separately to different people)
Slide 1 states the problem and must work completely alone. Slide 2 agitates it and must also stand
alone. Assume the reader sees only slide 1. If slide 1 needs slide 2 to make sense, rewrite slide 1.
