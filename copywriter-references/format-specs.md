# Format Specs

> The proven shape of each format, the craft note for each move, and the pass/fail test each draft
> must clear. These are the container. The member's voice, story, numbers and reframe are the contents.
> Read craft-fundamentals.md and hook-library.md alongside this. Build the checks in, never name them
> at the reader, never show this scaffolding in the final copy.

> WEAK vs STRONG: under each move, the weak shape is what a lazy or generic draft does, and the strong
> shape is the move done well. These teach the skill to recognise its own weak output and fix it before
> the member sees it. They are SHAPES, not lines to copy. Fill with the member's real material.

---

## /newsletter · the relationship email
Purpose: build trust. It deposits goodwill, it does not sell. Most of what the member sends should be
these, so that when they do sell, people listen. (The bank-account logic: every email is a deposit or
a withdrawal. You can only withdraw what you have put in.)

The Value Letter, in 5 steps:

1. SUBJECT + PREVIEW. See hook-library.md for patterns. 25-55 chars, one job: curiosity.
   - Weak shape: announces the topic ("This week's newsletter: comparison").
   - Strong shape: opens a gap in her own language; preview extends it, never repeats it.

2. OPEN ON A MOMENT, NOT A THESIS. A scene, a felt sensation, a confession.
   - Weak shape: a general statement of the topic. "I wanted to talk about comparison today."
   - Strong shape: drops her into a lived scene with a time and a body sensation. If the first line
     could open a memo, rewrite it as a moment.

3. NAME AN ENEMY THAT IS NOT HER. Reframe the problem as an outside system, not her effort or worth.
   - Weak shape: advice that blames her. "You just need to stop comparing and focus on your path."
   - Strong shape: lifts the blame and names a real hidden cause she had not seen. (Writing instruction,
     not a phrase for her. Never literally write "the enemy is." Never the "it's not X, it's Y" cliche.
     Find it in her words. See craft-fundamentals.md section 4 for how to find the reframe.)

4. SAY THE THING SHE FEELS BUT CANNOT WORD. The real 3am thought. Diagnose, do not describe.
   - Weak shape: a label for the feeling. "It can feel discouraging."
   - Strong shape: the exact spiralling questions in her head, in her words. This is the line that
     makes her feel read.

5. HAND HER SOMETHING REAL AND USABLE, FREE, INSIDE THE EMAIL. A clear, numbered way of thinking about
   the problem (3-6 parts, each a phrase she could repeat), ideally named.
   - Weak shape: a vague truth that gives her nothing. "Successful people have better systems."
   - Strong shape: a named, numbered framework she could screenshot and use today. If the member has
     no framework, interview until one is named from how they actually work. Never invent one and
     attribute it to them. No real value handed over = the email is not ready.

6. PROVE IT HONESTLY. A real number AND a real admission or messy detail. (craft-fundamentals.md 5.)

7. TURN IT ONTO HER. Hand the lesson back as one direct question and invite a reply.
   - Weak shape: "Let me know if this resonates!"
   - Strong shape: one specific, genuine question + "hit reply and tell me." If selling at all, only
     a short P.S., the way a friend recommends something.

PASS/FAIL (all true before she sees it):
- [ ] Opens on a concrete moment, not a general statement.
- [ ] Hands over one genuinely usable thing for free.
- [ ] Ends in a real question that invites a reply, not "let me know if this resonates."
- [ ] Reframe present and written in the member's voice, NOT the banned construction.
- [ ] No banned phrases, no em dashes. Editing pass done (see bottom).

## /sales-email · sell with a sequence, not a single send
Purpose: turn warmth into one action. Ask whether the member wants the full launch sequence or one
single email. Default is the 5-Email Launch Sequence, each email with one job:

1. ANNOUNCE. What is opening and who it is for. An invitation, not an announcement. Identity before pitch.
   - Weak shape: "Our program is now open for enrollment."
   - Strong shape: makes the reader feel chosen/accepted before any price is mentioned.

2. HANDLE THE CONCERN. Name the single biggest reason they hesitate and answer it head on, then reframe
   what staying still is costing.
   - Weak shape: ignore the objection and say "spots are filling up."
   - Strong shape: say the objection out loud before they can, then reframe the real cost of waiting.

3. ANSWER THE QUESTIONS. The real FAQ (time, format, "will this work for someone like me").
   - Weak shape: skip it and hope. An unanswered question is a silent no.
   - Strong shape: the five questions they actually ask every time, answered plainly.

4. PROOF. Testimonials and real results, chosen to show DIFFERENT kinds of people winning so everyone
   sees themselves. (Real only. Never invent a testimonial.)

5. CLOSE THE DOORS. A real deadline or real limit. Only ever real urgency.
   - Weak shape: a fake countdown. It works once, then burns the trust the newsletters built.
   - Strong shape: a true deadline that actually arrives. Name the real date or real cap.

Always: one email, one job, one link; value before the ask; match the ask to how warm they are;
value-framed first-person CTA ("Save my spot," not "Submit"), repeated after the value.

PASS/FAIL:
- [ ] One job and one link per email.
- [ ] The urgency named is real. If it is not real, it is not in the email.
- [ ] Every claim is the member's real number or result. Nothing invented.
- [ ] CTA is value-framed and first-person. No banned phrases, no em dashes. Editing pass done.

## /sales-page · long-form page, or improve an existing one
First ask: new page, or improving one they have? If improving, read their page and diagnose it against
the flow before changing anything. If new, lock these five inputs first: the pain in her words; the
reframe (the hidden cause she cannot see, in the member's words, not a stock phrase); the named method;
proof (2-3 real numbers with timeframes); the one outcome the page promises.

The 8 blocks, with the craft note for each:

1. HOOK. Outcome or identity headline (who she becomes, never a product description), plus the named
   method and a first CTA. (See hook-library.md.)
   - Weak shape: describes the product. "Join our membership."
   - Strong shape: sells the destination. "[Who she becomes]." She wants the after before she reads on.

2. THE PAIN. "Does this sound like you?" in her words, then the three big payoffs.
   - Weak shape: a generic list of problems. Strong shape: her specific private loop, named so exactly
     she feels seen. Diagram her actual cycle if it has one.

3. IMAGINE IF. The after, the specific opposite of the pain. Specific and daily, not abstract.

4. REFRAME AND STORY. The real hidden cause she had not seen, plus the member's "I have been there"
   story with proof numbers. The reframe is the spine of the page. (craft-fundamentals.md 4.) Story
   transfers authority; the reframe makes the method the logical fix.

5. INTRODUCE THE NAMED METHOD as a system with named parts, not a pile of features.

6. WHAT YOU GET + PROOF. Features now, each tied to an outcome (the so-what ladder). A value stack to a
   big honest anchor, then the real price and payment options. Testimonials: named, number-backed, and
   chosen to show different people winning.
   - Weak shape: a feature list with no outcomes, an inflated anchor. Strong shape: every feature ladders
     to who she becomes; the anchor is defensible; the price lands after the value, not before.

7. WHO IT IS FOR. "This is for you if" bullets, so the right person leans in and the wrong one leaves.
   Telling the wrong person it is not for them makes the right person trust you more.

8. OFFER, GUARANTEE, FAQ. Guarantee scaled to price; honest urgency (real seats, real dates); 5-8 real
   objections each ending in a CTA; a final close tied to who she becomes.

Benefits before features, always. Lead with "you," keep "I" for proof, a number on every benefit.

PASS/FAIL:
- [ ] The hook sells who she becomes, not what the product is.
- [ ] Block 4 has a real reframe AND a real founder story. No invented backstory.
- [ ] Every feature ladders to an outcome. Every testimonial and number is real.
- [ ] No em dashes anywhere. Editing pass done.

## /social · captions, hooks and posts for the platform they're on
First establish the JOB: Build (deepen trust with existing followers), Grow (reach new people), or
Monetize (move them off-platform into the member's world). One job per post. Then the Content Formula:
Hook, Problem, Solution, Proof, one CTA. Act as a current expert on the named platform; if unsure of
the latest, say what to check rather than guessing.

1. HOOK. Most of the game. About three seconds. (See hook-library.md for patterns and failure modes.)
   - Weak shape: "Here are some tips for growing your business."
   - Strong shape: her exact words, a specific number, or an honest confession that stops the scroll.

2. PROBLEM. Name the thing she feels, fast, in her words.
3. SOLUTION. The actual shift or steps, not a tease.
4. PROOF. A number, a result, a real example, so it is not just a claim. (Real only.)
5. CTA. One thing to do next, matched to the post's job. (Monetize: comment the keyword / link in bio.)

Craft notes:
- Start from her one person and say the most potent thing in the fewest words. Cut everything that is
  not the most potent thing.
- Write from the member's lived experience, not generic expertise. Pull a real moment out of them first.
- Keep them human: a typo, all caps, a real aside. It should read like them, not a brand.
- Carousel: slide 1 and slide 2 must each stand completely alone (platform shows them separately).

PASS/FAIL:
- [ ] The post's one job is clear and the CTA matches it.
- [ ] The hook works in the first 3 seconds, standing alone.
- [ ] Uses a real moment or number from the member, nothing invented.
- [ ] No banned phrases, no em dashes. Editing pass done.

## WHAT THE DOMINO BELIEF DOES IN EACH FORMAT
Read `domino-belief.md` first, every time. Name the job before you draft. Never state the belief
outright in the copy: it is the spine, not the sentence.

- **/social** · every post moves the belief one inch. It proves it with one story, one example, one
  reframe. One post, one inch. Never the whole argument.
- **/newsletter** · the same job with room to breathe. The belief is the through-line and the letter
  is the evidence: something that actually happened, landing on why it means what the belief says.
- **/sales-email** · by now she is on the list, so the belief is assumed. These emails answer the
  "yeah, but" objections that come off it. Find them by saying the belief out loud and listening for
  what someone would push back with.
- **/sales-page** · the belief appears once, explicitly, after the promise and the story and before
  the offer. It is the "why this way and not the other way" section, and most pages are missing it.

## THE EDITING PASS (run on EVERY draft, every format, before the member sees it)
Good copy is rewritten, not written. After drafting and passing the format test, do a second pass:

1. CUT. Remove every word that does not earn its place. Aim to cut 15-20% without losing meaning.
   Throat-clearing, warm-up sentences, restated ideas, hedge words: gone.
2. STRENGTHEN VERBS. Replace weak verb + adverb with one strong verb. Replace "is/are" constructions
   with active ones where it tightens the line.
3. VARY RHYTHM. No two long sentences back to back. A short line after a long one lands harder. Read it
   aloud in your head; if it drones, break the rhythm.
4. FRONT-LOAD. Put the most interesting words at the start of the sentence and the section. She skims.
5. BELIEF CHECK. Would this piece work just as well for a competitor with a different belief? If
   yes, it is generic. Find the line that only this member could have written, and build around it.
6. SPECIFICITY SWEEP. Find every vague word ("success," "growth," "amazing") and replace with a concrete
   detail or a real number, or cut it. If a number is needed and not available, ask the member.
6. THE "SAYS A LOT, SAYS NOTHING" TEST. Read each paragraph back. If you cannot reduce it to one plain,
   useful sentence, it is filler. Cut or rewrite it.
7. FINAL VOICE CHECK. Run the banned list and em dash rule one more time. Does it sound like the member
   read it aloud, or like a brand wrote it? If the latter, it is not done.
