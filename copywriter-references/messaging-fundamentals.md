# Messaging Fundamentals

> The layer ABOVE the words. craft-fundamentals.md teaches how a sentence lands.
> This file decides what the piece is for at all. Get this wrong and good writing does not save it.
> Every command reads this FIRST, before craft-fundamentals.md.

---

## 1. Two questions come before any writing

- **Who is this for?** Specific enough that she reads two lines and thinks "this is me."
- **What does she need to do?** One action or one decision. Not three.

If either is missing, ask the member. Do not write around it.

Test: could the wrong person keep reading comfortably? Then the who is too wide.

---

## 2. The domino belief

**The one thing SHE has to believe before anything the member sells makes sense.**

It is a belief held by the reader, about the member's vehicle. It is never a belief about the member
herself. Its job is to turn a want she already has into a need for this specific thing.

**The formula: VEHICLE + SUPERLATIVE = TRANSFORMATION.**

Written out: "[vehicle] is the [superlative] way to [transformation she already wants]."

- **Vehicle** is what she needs. The method, the approach, the thing the member actually teaches.
  The one line to hold on to: **the vehicle is what she needs, the transformation is what she
  already wants.** Put what she wants on the left and there is no belief, only a wish.
- **Transformation** is what she already wants. Something she wanted this morning, before she had
  ever heard of the member.
- **Superlative** is what makes it provocative: the only, the fastest, the most effective, the one
  thing. Without it there is no belief. "A good way to" is not a claim, it is a hedge, and it makes
  every other option stay on the table. Say it in a way that makes the alternatives fall away.

Worked example: "YouTube is the most effective way to build an engaged audience online."
Not: "YouTube is a good way to grow."

**What it is NOT. Reject these and ask again:**

- The member's own origin story, or what the member personally stopped believing. That is founder
  material for `voice-profile.md`, and it is the most common wrong answer. It describes a change in
  the member, not a claim about the reader's world.
- A value, a slogan, or a tagline.
- Anything nobody could argue with. If no reasonable person disagrees, it is not a belief.
- Anything that only makes sense to someone who already bought.
- **The member's own programme as the vehicle.** "My Signature Method is the only way to grow" makes
  her sound arrogant and it sells nothing. The vehicle is the approach, never the packaging. A named
  framework only works when the name itself is intriguing.
- **Two vehicles stacked together.** One belief, one vehicle. Stacking dilutes it.
- **A feature dressed as a vehicle.** How the member personalises the work (a framework, a chart, a
  profile, a diagnostic) is not the vehicle. It is how the vehicle is made to fit her. Features go
  in the pitch, never in the domino.
- **A step inside the mechanism, instead of the mechanism.** This is the most common bad option and
  it looks reasonable, which is why it slips through. "Writing your business down", "getting
  organised", "batching your content" are all things you do ON THE WAY. The test: strip away
  everything else the member teaches and keep only this one thing. Does it still produce the
  transformation on its own? If yes, it is the vehicle. If it is one of five steps, it is a pillar,
  and pillars belong in the pitch as modules, never in the domino.
- **Something she could already do alone.** If she can do it this Sunday with a notebook and no
  help, agreeing with the belief does not make her want the member. Drop that half and keep the half
  she genuinely cannot do without them. A thing she has tried and abandoned is the strongest signal
  here: she does not need to be told to do it again, she needs the thing that makes it work.

**How to build it, in this order:**

1. Ask what her person already wants, in that person's words. Do not accept an abstraction.
   That is the transformation.
2. Find the vehicle: the mechanism the member teaches, the one you could put on a sign. Not a step
   inside it, not a feature, not the programme name. Before proposing any option, run the strip-away
   test on it yourself and discard the ones that fail. Only show her options that survive.
3. Pick the superlative that is honestly true: the only, the fastest, the most effective, the one thing.
4. Join them into the formula and read it back.
5. **Cut it.** Almost every first attempt is too long. If a clause can be moved somewhere else in the
   messaging, move it. Say plainly what you are cutting and where it goes instead.

**The six-point test. Run all six out loud, do not skip any:**

1. Relevant to what she actually sells.
2. Clear and simple, fourth-grade words. Clear is not the same as fully understood. A gap is fine,
   and the gap is often why she stays.
3. Positive and outcome-oriented, never the negative version.
4. Provocative. If nobody could raise an eyebrow at it, it is boring, and boring is the real failure.
5. Does agreeing with it make her want to buy, or could she act on it alone this weekend? If she
   could do it alone, the vehicle is too easy.
6. Would she still be able to recite it in six months?

What it changes: free content sells the belief, paid work delivers on it. Teach the what, sell the how.

If `domino-belief.md` is not in the brain, ask for it before writing anything persuasive.
If it is there but says STATUS: NOT_YET, she parked it on purpose. Write the piece anyway and mention
it once at the end, kindly. Never block her and never ask twice.
Capture it in her words. Never invent one.

## 3. Lead with the promise

- **Process** is what is inside: modules, calls, weeks, the community.
- **Promise** is what she can do afterwards that she cannot do now.

The promise leads, always. The process comes after, and it earns its place once she wants the outcome.

- Weak: "Six modules, four live calls and a private community."
- Strong: "By the end you walk away with [the thing]."

The shape that works: **by the end of this you are going to walk away with ___.** Fill the blank with
something she can hold, not a mood. A feeling ("you will feel calmer about your business") is not a
promise. A thing she now has, or can now do, is.

**State the promise in the positive.** Say what she WILL have, be, or do. A promise written as an
absence ("you will never write a caption on a Sunday night again") makes her picture the problem
rather than the outcome. Rewrite it as what she now has. This applies to every promise, headline
and CTA the member writes.

And never try to argue someone into wanting something. Copy connects what the member has to a want
that already exists. If the piece is working hard to make her care, that is a strategy problem, not a
copy problem, and say so plainly.

---

## 4. The gate before drafting

Four things must exist before a draft does:

1. **Who** it is for.
2. **What** she must do.
3. **The belief** the piece moves.
4. **The promise**, in outcome language.

Missing one? Ask one question and get it. Do not guess and do not draft around it.
