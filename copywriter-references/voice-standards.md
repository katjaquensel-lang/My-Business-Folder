# Voice Standards: Banned Words and Mechanical Rules

> Quality by constraint, not by adjective. It is more reliable to say what never to write
> than to say "write well." Every command checks every draft against this file before you see it.
> This is the STARTER list. During setup you added your own hated words and removed any that
> are actually part of your voice. Keep editing it forever.

## The em dash rule (absolute)
No em dashes (—) anywhere in body copy. Ever. Rewrite the sentence. This is non-negotiable and
is the single fastest way to spot AI writing.

## Banned phrases (the AI-giveaway kill-list)
These are the tells that make copy sound machine-written. Never use them.

### Category 1 · AI giveaway constructions
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X, it's about Y" (the load-bearing reframe cliche)
- "Here's the thing" / "Here's the kicker" / "But here's the truth"
- "Let that sink in" / "Read that again"
- "In a world where..."
- "Whether you're X or Y..."
- "Imagine a world where" / "Picture this"
- Stacked one-line fragments for fake drama (a line. then another line. then another.)
- Three-item parallel fragments ("Clearer. Faster. Stronger.") used as a closer
- A neat aphorism as the final line to feel profound

### Category 2 · Corporate filler
- "leverage," "utilize," "synergy," "robust," "seamless," "streamline"
- "best-in-class," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "next-level," "game-changer"
- "unlock," "supercharge," "elevate," "empower," "transform" (as filler)
- "delve," "dive deep," "deep dive," "tap into," "harness"
- "in today's fast-paced world," "now more than ever"

### Category 3 · Hedge and filler transitions
- "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "That being said," "At the end of the day"
- "It's important to note that," "It goes without saying," "Needless to say"
- "very," "really," "actually," "literally," "just" (when they add nothing)

### Category 4 · Empty enthusiasm
- "I'm so excited to share," "I'm thrilled to announce," "We're delighted to"
- Exclamation points as a substitute for a strong sentence
- "amazing," "incredible," "awesome," "epic" as the only descriptor

## Mechanical rules (measurable, enforceable)
- Sentence length: mostly 10-20 words. Vary it. Never two long sentences back to back.
- Active voice at least 90% of the time.
- No semicolons in body copy.
- Reading level around sixth grade. Short words beat long ones.
- One idea per sentence. One idea per paragraph for sales copy.
- Paragraphs of 2-4 sentences in long copy, so it survives a phone screen.
- Lead with "you." Keep "I" for proof and story.
- Put a real number on a benefit wherever one exists.
- Contractions on (write like you talk), unless the member's voice profile says otherwise.

**Banned habits (German):** excessive emojis; long, rambling storytelling with no clear point
(her own confirmed habit, to watch for); text with no clear, concrete, actionable point at the
end; big words that mean nothing; filler for word count; forced metaphors; put-on, saccharine
empathy; unprompted bullet lists; listicle openers like "Hier sind 5 Wege, um..."; saying "as
an AI..."; ending every paragraph with a question; the hedged "einerseits, andererseits" that
reads like a stock chatbot; "ich versuche" or other hedging instead of a clear, confident
statement, even when Katja doesn't feel confident in the moment — the writing shows confidence
regardless of how she feels.

**Katja's own pet peeve (from Week 1, still standing):** the generic body-check-in phrase "Wie
fühlt sich das für dich an?" and similar. Not because feeling doesn't matter (it's the actual
core of the work), but because the phrase itself is a worn-out spiritual-coach cliché. Say
concrete body sensations and feelings instead, grounded, never floaty.

**Word choice, not a ban but binding:** "begleiten" (to accompany/guide), never "helfen" (to
help) or similar tired coaching language, wherever it fits and feels real.
